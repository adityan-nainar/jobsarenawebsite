EP 6 · Claim checkUpdated 17 Nov 2026

“AI will create 170 million jobs.” Here’s the full list.

The 170 million comes from the World Economic Forum’s Future of Jobs Report 2025. It counts every big trend together. AI’s own share is far smaller. Below: the jobs the report says will grow and shrink by 2030.

Read the WEF chapterJump to: the lists
Misleading

170 million is every trend combined. AI’s line is +11M, −9M.

The report is a survey of employers’ expectations, not a count of jobs that exist. Treat every number here as a forecast.

1 · The headline numbers

170 million created, 92 million displaced

Employers expect the big trends of 2025–2030 to create about 170 million jobs and displace about 92 million, a net gain of about 78 million. Creation equals about 14% of today’s jobs, displacement about 8%.

WEF, Future of Jobs Report 2025 · grade C

2 · AI’s share

AI: about 11 million created, 9 million displaced

AI and information processing technologies are expected to create about 11 million jobs and displace about 9 million, a net gain of about 2 million. The largest net creator is broadening digital access (+19M, −9M). The largest net destroyer is robots and autonomous systems (about 5 million fewer jobs).

WEF, Future of Jobs Report 2025 · grade C

3 · Growing

Jobs growing by 2030

Most new jobs (in numbers):

  1. Farmworkers, about +35 million. Already more than 200 million people do this job.
  2. Delivery drivers
  3. Building construction workers
  4. Salespersons
  5. Food processing workers
  6. Nursing professionals
  7. Social work and counselling professionals
  8. Personal care aides
  9. University and higher education teachers
  10. Secondary education teachers
  11. Software and applications developers
  12. General and operations managers
  13. Project managers

The report gives a number only for farmworkers. The others are listed in the order the report’s text names them, which is not necessarily their rank.

Growing fastest (in percentage): big data specialists, FinTech engineers, AI and machine learning specialists, software and applications developers, and security management specialists lead. Information security analysts, autonomous and electric vehicle specialists, environmental engineers and renewable energy engineers are also in the top 15.

WEF, Future of Jobs Report 2025, chapter 2 · grade C

4 · Shrinking

Jobs shrinking by 2030

Shrinking fastest (in percentage):

  1. Cashiers and ticket clerks
  2. Administrative assistants and executive secretaries
  3. Printing workers
  4. Accountants and auditors

Graphic designers and legal secretaries sit just outside the top 10. Most jobs lost (in numbers): clerical and secretarial workers.

The report names only these in its text. The full rankings are in its charts (figures 2.2 to 2.5): see the report.

WEF, Future of Jobs Report 2025, chapter 2 · grade C

Do this

Find your job’s tasks, not just its title.

  1. Find your job above. Growing, shrinking, or not listed?
  2. List your weekly tasks. Mark the ones that are routine data entry, formatting or checking. Those are the parts most exposed in shrinking roles like clerks and accountants.
  3. Move toward the growing side of your own job. For an analyst: more judgment, client conversations and checking AI’s work; less manual reconciliation.
  4. Learn one tool from the growing list’s world (data, AI, security) well enough to use it in your current work.

Our own advice, not from the report · grade D.

What this doesn’t show

These are employers’ expectations from a survey, not a measurement. The job data covers about 1.18 billion workers, a part of global employment, so it doesn’t describe every country or sector. Farmworker growth is mostly about other trends, not AI.
